Remember the casting-call paper?

Senior author: Dawn Song. Berkeley professor, MacArthur “genius,” crypto-startup founder and, since June, a Meta vice president of AI research.

How we read the comments

We captured 475 ordinary-account comments and replies from a 5:27 screen recording, made September 26, of the thread under the remix; the platform's counter read 534 to 542 during the recording, and some threads stayed collapsed. Names, handles and faces are removed; bracketed text replaces names.

Stance (what the comment says the video is): one AI coder assigned a single stance from its wording: said AI (91, including 29 implied), said real (16), remake or wig (15), just asked (8), or no stance (345: 124 remarks, 76 tags, 55 emoji, 49 quotes, 41 images). There was no second coder for stance.

Reaction (how the comment responds): three independent AI coding passes labeled every comment as delight (enjoyment, amusement or praise of the video or its craft), alarm (worry, fear or distress about AI, deception or telling what's real), both, or neither; laughter aimed at another commenter counted as neither. Majority vote decided each label; the passes were unanimous on 468 of 475 (Fleiss' κ = 0.98). Result: 114 delight, 20 alarm, 0 both, 341 neither. An AI reread all 20 alarm labels and a sample of the rest against the comments; no human second pass. High agreement among automated coders can reflect shared assumptions, so treat this as a careful reading of words, not a measure of what viewers believed.

TikTok's timestamps showed Tako's replies as posted about 20 hours, 19 hours and 53 minutes before our recording; the first reply's final sentences were read from the frames. We didn't recreate the exchange. The replies show inconsistency, not intent; nothing suggests Tako meant to deceive anyone.

The paper, our four questions, and what they don't say

Wenjie Qu, Xuandong Zhao, Jiaheng Zhang and Dawn Song, “Self-Sovereign Agent,” preprint dated March 16, 2026 (National University of Singapore and UC Berkeley). Definition and properties: §2. Levels 1–4: §3. Economic, replication and adaptation loops, crypto-native payments and offspring wallet models: §4 and Figure 3. Challenges: §5. Legal accountability, labor, agents as employers and security drift: §6. Alternative views: §7. Related decentralized agents, including Truth Terminal and meme-coin monetization: §8. The authors restrict their analysis to agents with fixed, developer-set goals and assume no machine consciousness (§§8–9). The paper does not discuss Jean Phil.

The paper was submitted to arXiv on March 4, 2026 and posted publicly in April (arXiv:2604.08551 ↗); the PDF on the project page is dated March 16. Author details: MacArthur Foundation ↗ (Song, 2010); Song's bio ↗ (AMiner's most-cited recognition, research areas); MIT Technology Review ↗ on Oasis Labs (2018); Axios, via Yahoo Finance ↗, on her joining Meta Superintelligence Labs (June 25, 2026); co-authors' research from their own pages (Zhang: zero-knowledge proofs for machine learning; Qu and Zhao: watermarking AI-generated text and images).

The four questions are our device. They paraphrase the paper's four properties (§2: operational independence, resource autonomy, persistence, adaptive capability). Our answers for Jean Phil are our reporting; “itself” describes the paper's hypothetical agent, not Jean Phil. Mapping the levels onto characters, remixers and coins is our editorial application, and the simulation is a toy illustration of the λspawn > λtakedown race; because copies crowd each other out, its displayed remix rate is attempts, not successful spawns. Truth Terminal details come from TechCrunch (Dec. 19, 2024).

How we made our replication

Our character sheet was generated from three @jean_philanthrope videos. Following the approach in Ty Farrago's tutorial, we ran Higgsfield Genjutsu Object Swap on three overlapping sections of the donkey clip and assembled them to the original timeline (64.23 seconds, 720 × 1280, 30 fps). The generated footage runs to 60.23 seconds, followed by the original closing card. The source audio is copied unchanged. Hands, contact with the animal and some facial details visibly distort. Apart from this page, we have not posted it anywhere, and we have observed no audience response. Every frame of our copy carries an “AI face swap” label; the version on this page is compressed to 576 × 1024.
